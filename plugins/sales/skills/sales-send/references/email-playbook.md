# Email playbook — first touches and follow-ups

## The shape of a first email

```
Subject: <their signal, in their words — 3 to 6 words, no "quick question">

<Name> — <one line: the reason now (their posting / round / post, with when) or the shared
history the brain holds (the last thread, the person you both know)>.

<one line: the problem that reason usually means, in the buyer's words>. <one proof point from
the offer profile, with its number only if the profile has it>.

<one question they can answer in a line>?

<signature>
Reply 'no thanks' and I won't write again.
```

- Under 120 words. Plain text. No images, no tracking, no calendar link in a first email.
- Their words, not ours: lift the term from their posting or post.
- One question. "Worth a look?" is not a question about them; "How are you handling X today?" is.

## Follow-ups

- Each follow-up adds one new thing: a detail tied to their signal, a short example, a different angle.
- Never "just bumping this", "circling back", "did you see my last email".
- The last step closes the loop: thanks, the door stays open, and — if they are not the owner —
  who is.

## Warm and revival

- Warm: the first line is the shared history, said plainly ("We last spoke at the March meetup").
- Revival: name the old deal and the reason it stopped (from the deal note); say what changed.
  Never pretend the gap did not happen.

## Intro asks (to the connector)

- Why this person, why now, and a two-line blurb the connector can forward unchanged.
- An easy no: "If it's not a fit, no need to reply."

## Lines the linter will stop

- A number not in the offer profile or materials.
- A do-not-claim term.
- A `[CONFIRM]` marker or a `{{placeholder}}`.
- A missing opt-out line.
