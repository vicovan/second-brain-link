> The two or three kinds of role this person targets, and how each one is argued. The scout maps
> every job to one of these by its trigger keywords and **drops any job that matches none**;
> cv-tailor builds the headline, summary and bullet order from the matching entry. A recruiter who
> sees one person apply across five unrelated role types reads none of them seriously — the
> archetypes are the discipline that prevents it.

# Target archetypes

## <archetype-key>  ·  <plain name, e.g. Engineering leadership>

- **Target titles:** the exact titles this lane searches, most senior first.
- **Trigger keywords:** words in a title or JD that place a job in this lane.
- **Not this lane:** titles that look similar but need different evidence (e.g. a hands-on
  research role wearing a "Head of" title).
- **Evidence strength:** strong · moderate · thin — how much of the profile genuinely supports
  it. A thin lane gets fewer applications, because the shortlist-likelihood floor filters it.
- **Headline pattern:** `<target title> · <one fact>`, ≤ 80 characters — the posting's title
  first, then one concrete differentiator. Never a second title or a list of domains.
- **Summary skeleton:** three or four plain sentences — who this person is for this lane (the
  current role first for employee lanes), the proof that leads, the scale credential that closes.
  Never a "<Title> profile:" label or credentials glued with semicolons.
- **Lead proof points:** the three or four facts from `profile.md` §4/§6 that open this lane's CVs.
- **Bullet priority:** which roles get depth, which collapse to one line.
- **Why-angles:** two or three true reasons this person wants roles of this kind — the raw
  material for "why this role" answers, combined each time with a fact about the employer.
- **Stories:** IDs from `stories.md` that fit this lane's behavioural questions.
- **Framing policy:** how a current venture and overlapping roles appear (profile §9).
